## Overview

A dental implant and a dental bridge are both options for replacing a missing tooth, but they work differently, involve different procedures, and have different cost and longevity profiles. Understanding how they compare helps patients have a more informed conversation with their provider before committing to either.

## How Each Works

### Dental Implant

A dental implant replaces the missing tooth by placing a titanium post directly into the jawbone, where it integrates with bone through osseointegration. After healing, an abutment and crown are attached to create a restoration that looks, feels, and functions like a natural tooth. The implant is a standalone restoration that does not rely on adjacent teeth.

### Dental Bridge

A dental bridge replaces a missing tooth by spanning the gap between two adjacent teeth (the abutment teeth) and suspending a prosthetic tooth (the pontic) in between. To support the bridge, the adjacent teeth must be filed down and capped with crowns. The bridge is fixed — it is cemented in place and not removable by the patient.

## Key Differences

| Factor | Dental Implant | Dental Bridge |
|---|---|---|  
| Adjacent teeth involved | No | Yes — must be prepared |
| Bone preservation | Yes — implant stimulates bone | No — bone under pontic resorbs |
| Longevity | Often 15+ years with maintenance | Often 10–15 years |
| Procedure time | Months (osseointegration) | Weeks (no healing period) |
| Cost (initial) | Higher | Lower |
| Adjacent tooth risk | None | Prepared teeth are permanently altered |

## When an Implant May Be Preferred

An implant may be the preferred option when:

- adjacent teeth are healthy and should not be altered
- long-term bone preservation is important
- the patient is a good surgical candidate with adequate bone
- the patient prefers a standalone solution with implant longevity

## When a Bridge May Be Preferred

A bridge may be considered when:

- the adjacent teeth already have crowns or significant restorations
- bone volume is insufficient for implant placement and grafting is not desired
- the patient cannot undergo surgery due to systemic health factors
- cost or timeline is a primary consideration

## What the Provider Assessment Should Include

The choice between an implant and a bridge involves individual clinical factors including bone volume, the condition of adjacent teeth, systemic health, and the patient's preferences and timeline. A provider should explain both options and the rationale for a recommendation.

## Questions to Ask

- What are the advantages and disadvantages of each option in my specific case?
- Would the adjacent teeth need to be altered for a bridge?
- Is my bone volume adequate for an implant, or would grafting be needed?
- What is the expected longevity of each option?
- What are the cost differences for the full treatment — not just the initial procedure?
