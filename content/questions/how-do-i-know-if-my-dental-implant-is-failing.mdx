## Introduction

Dental implants have a high success rate overall, but they can fail — either during the osseointegration period or years after successful integration. Understanding the warning signs at each stage, and knowing when to contact a provider, helps New York patients respond appropriately and avoid delayed treatment of a developing problem.

---

## Early Failure: During Osseointegration

Early implant failure occurs when osseointegration does not complete successfully. This typically happens within the first 3–6 months after placement. Signs include:

- **Implant mobility** — The implant feels loose or shifts under gentle pressure. Any perceptible movement in an implant is abnormal and warrants immediate evaluation.
- **Persistent or worsening pain** — Some discomfort in the days after surgery is expected. Pain that worsens after day 3–4, or that returns after initial improvement, is a warning sign.
- **Persistent swelling or discharge** — Swelling that does not resolve within the first week, or discharge from the implant site, suggests possible infection.
- **Implant feeling higher than adjacent teeth** — An implant that feels raised in the bite may be mobile.

Early failure can result from infection at the time of placement, insufficient bone-implant contact, contamination, or systemic factors affecting healing.

---

## Late Failure: After Successful Integration

Late implant failure occurs after the implant has successfully integrated and has been in function. Common causes include:

**Peri-implantitis** — Infection of the tissue and bone surrounding the implant, similar in mechanism to periodontal disease around natural teeth. Signs include:
- Bleeding or discharge around the implant when pressed
- Visible gum recession exposing the implant surface
- Bone loss visible on imaging
- Pain or tenderness around an implant that was previously asymptomatic

**Occlusal overload** — Excessive bite force on the implant, from grinding, clenching, or a crown that is too high, can cause bone loss and eventual implant loosening.

**Crown or abutment failure** — The crown or abutment can crack, loosen, or fracture without the implant itself failing. These are restorative issues, not implant failures, but may be mistaken for implant problems.

---

## What Is Not a Sign of Implant Failure

Not every discomfort indicates implant failure. Normal healing involves:

- Soreness and swelling in the first week after surgery
- Mild sensitivity to pressure at the implant site during osseointegration
- Mild gum tenderness when oral hygiene is resumed around the implant

If you are uncertain whether what you are experiencing is normal, contact your provider — they can assess without exposing you to unnecessary concern.

---

## When to Contact Your Provider

Contact your provider promptly (within 24–48 hours) if you notice:

- Any mobility or movement in the implant
- Pain that is worsening, not improving, beyond day 3 after surgery
- Swelling that is spreading or not resolving after the first week
- Discharge or unusual taste from the implant site
- A previously stable implant that now feels different

Any of these warrants evaluation. Peri-implantitis is more treatable when caught early; delayed treatment increases the risk of bone loss that cannot be reversed without grafting.

---

## What Providers Do When a Failing Implant Is Suspected

- Clinical assessment of implant stability
- Imaging to assess bone level around the implant
- If early failure is confirmed: the implant is typically removed, the site is allowed to heal, and reimplantation may be considered after a review of contributing factors
- If peri-implantitis is present: treatment ranges from deep cleaning around the implant to surgical intervention, depending on severity

---

## Questions to Ask Before Implant Placement

- What is your protocol for monitoring implant stability during the osseointegration period?
- How do I reach you or your team if I have concerns between scheduled appointments?
- What are the most common reasons implants fail in cases like mine, and how are those risks managed?
