## Introduction

General dentists perform many dental surgical procedures, including simple extractions. Oral surgeons — oral maxillofacial surgeons (OMFS) — are specialists whose scope extends into surgical complexity, bone surgery, sedation administration, and medical-dental crossover cases. Understanding when a general dentist's scope is sufficient and when a specialist is appropriate helps New York patients evaluate referrals and make better decisions about provider selection.

---

## What General Dentists Can Perform

A licensed general dentist in New York may perform:

- Simple extractions of visible, single-rooted teeth with straightforward anatomy
- Some surgical extractions, depending on training and experience
- Basic oral surgery procedures within their training scope
- Local anesthesia and, in some cases, minimal sedation with appropriate certification

General dentists vary significantly in surgical experience and scope. Some have extensive surgical volume; others refer most extractions beyond simple cases. When a general dentist proposes a procedure at the edge of typical scope, asking about their surgical volume and specific training for that procedure type is reasonable.

---

## When an Oral Surgeon Is the More Appropriate Provider

Referral to an oral surgeon is typically indicated when one or more of the following applies:

**Impacted teeth** — Teeth that are fully or partially beneath the gum line or embedded in bone require surgical extraction distinctly different from simple removal. Impaction type and depth affect complexity significantly.

**Multiple extractions in one session** — Extracting several teeth simultaneously, particularly under sedation, is standard OMFS practice.

**IV sedation or general anesthesia is required or preferred** — General dentists may offer oral sedation or nitrous oxide, but IV sedation and general anesthesia in an office setting require specific OMFS anesthesia training and licensure. If the procedure warrants IV or general anesthesia, confirm the administering provider holds the appropriate credentials.

**Bone grafting** — Socket preservation, ridge augmentation, and sinus lifts are surgical bone procedures. Complex grafting — particularly sinus lifts and staged ridge augmentation — is commonly within OMFS scope.

**Dental implants involving surgical complexity** — Cases involving bone grafting, sinus anatomy, or proximity to nerves and anatomical structures are more clearly within OMFS scope than straightforward single implant placement.

**Infections with systemic involvement** — Dental abscesses that have spread to facial spaces, or are associated with swelling, fever, or difficulty swallowing, require immediate OMFS or emergency department evaluation.

**Jaw-related conditions** — TMJ disorders, jaw fractures, facial injuries, cysts, tumors, and biopsies are within OMFS specialty scope.

**Medical complexity** — Patients on anticoagulants, bisphosphonates, chemotherapy, or with compromised immune systems often benefit from OMFS management due to the medical-surgical component of the OMFS residency.

---

## How to Evaluate a Referral

If a general dentist refers you to a specialist, or if you are selecting a specialist independently, useful questions include:

- Is this procedure within general dentistry scope, or does it specifically require OMFS training?
- What is the anesthesia plan — and who administers it?
- Is the provider board-certified or board-eligible in oral and maxillofacial surgery?
- If implants or bone grafting are involved, what imaging has been done to assess anatomy?

---

## When a General Dentist's Approach Is Appropriate

Not every oral surgical procedure requires an OMFS referral. Simple extractions of visible teeth with straightforward anatomy in otherwise healthy patients are routinely performed by general dentists. The relevant question is whether case complexity matches what general dentistry scope typically covers — not whether any surgical procedure requires a specialist by default.
