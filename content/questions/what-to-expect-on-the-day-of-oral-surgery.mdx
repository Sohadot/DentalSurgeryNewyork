## Introduction

Knowing what to expect on the day of oral surgery helps patients prepare practically and reduces uncertainty. While specific protocols vary by provider and procedure, the general sequence of events at most oral surgery appointments in New York follows a recognizable pattern.

## Before You Arrive

Pre-surgical instructions are given in advance and vary depending on the type of anesthesia involved. For procedures involving IV sedation or general anesthesia, fasting — not eating or drinking for a specified window before surgery — is typically required. Patients should review these instructions carefully and contact the office if anything is unclear.

Arranging transportation is essential for any appointment involving sedation. Patients who receive sedation are not permitted to drive themselves home and typically cannot take public transit unaccompanied.

## At Check-In

Upon arrival, patients typically review and sign consent documentation that outlines the procedure, risks, and alternatives. If this documentation has not been reviewed in advance, time should be allocated to read it carefully. Questions about anything in the consent forms can be raised with the provider or clinical staff before the procedure begins.

Medical history and medication information are often confirmed at this point. Patients should bring a complete and current list of all medications, supplements, and any relevant health history.

## Before the Procedure

Depending on the type of sedation, clinical staff will prepare the patient — which may involve placing an IV line, applying monitoring equipment such as blood pressure and pulse oximetry, and reviewing the procedure with the patient. The provider may briefly explain what they will do, though a full consultation should have already occurred at a prior appointment.

## During the Procedure

For patients receiving IV sedation or general anesthesia, the procedure itself is often not consciously experienced. The depth of sedation varies by type: patients under conscious sedation may be aware of some activity but feel relaxed; patients under general anesthesia are unconscious during the procedure.

For procedures performed under local anesthesia only, patients remain awake and aware throughout.

## After the Procedure

A recovery period follows most procedures involving sedation. Patients are monitored until they are alert and stable enough for discharge. Clinical staff will provide discharge instructions covering what to expect in terms of swelling, bleeding, and discomfort, medications prescribed or recommended, diet and activity restrictions, and signs that warrant contacting the office or seeking emergency care.

## Questions to Ask in Advance

- What are the fasting requirements before my appointment?
- Who should accompany me, and how long should they plan to wait?
- When will I receive discharge instructions, and in what format?
- What is the after-hours contact process if I have concerns following the procedure?
