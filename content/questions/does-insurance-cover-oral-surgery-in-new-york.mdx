## Introduction

Insurance coverage for oral surgery in New York varies significantly by the type of procedure, the type of insurance policy, and how the claim is categorized. Coverage is not automatic, and many patients are surprised to learn what is and is not included after treatment has already begun.

## Dental Insurance vs. Medical Insurance

Oral surgery claims may fall under dental insurance, medical insurance, or both — depending on the nature of the procedure. This distinction is not purely a technicality; it affects which policy applies, what the deductible and coverage limits are, and what documentation the insurer requires.

**Dental insurance** typically covers procedures performed in a dental context, such as tooth extraction, wisdom tooth removal, and often some portion of bone grafting when it is deemed necessary. Coverage is subject to annual maximums, which can be relatively low.

**Medical insurance** may cover oral surgery procedures when the procedure has a medical necessity component — such as jaw reconstruction, treatment of infection, or surgery performed under general anesthesia in a hospital or surgical center. The criteria for medical necessity vary by insurer and plan.

## Procedures Commonly Subject to Coverage Questions

- **Wisdom tooth removal**: Often covered under dental insurance, but coverage varies by plan, impaction type, and whether sedation is included.
- **Tooth extraction**: Usually covered in part under dental insurance, but coverage amounts and applicable codes vary.
- **Dental implants**: Many dental insurance plans specifically exclude implants or cover only limited portions of treatment. Some plans cover extraction and bone grafting but not the implant itself.
- **Bone grafting**: Coverage depends on whether the graft is categorized as diagnostically necessary and which insurance plan applies.
- **Sedation**: Coverage for sedation fees is inconsistent. Some plans cover sedation when the procedure meets certain complexity thresholds; many do not.

## What to Do Before Treatment

Contact your insurer before scheduling treatment. Ask specifically:

- Is this procedure covered under my plan?
- What documentation does the provider need to submit for prior authorization?
- What is my deductible for this type of claim, and how much of it has been met?
- Is there an annual maximum that applies, and how much have I used?
- Is the provider in-network, and how does that affect reimbursement?

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

- Do you accept my insurance, and are you in-network?
- Will your office submit the claim on my behalf, or do I need to submit it directly?
- Can you provide a predetermination of benefits before treatment begins?
- How will the estimate change if insurance does not cover the procedure as expected?
