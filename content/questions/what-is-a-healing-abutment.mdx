## What Is a Healing Abutment?

A healing abutment — also called a healing cap or cover screw — is a temporary component placed on top of the dental implant post after initial osseointegration has occurred. Its purpose is to shape the gum tissue in preparation for the final abutment and crown.

Understanding where the healing abutment fits in the implant sequence helps patients follow their treatment timeline and know what to expect between the surgical phase and final restoration.

## Where It Fits in the Implant Sequence

The typical implant treatment sequence involves several stages:

1. **Implant post placement** — The titanium fixture is placed into the jawbone
2. **Osseointegration period** — 3 to 6 months (or longer) during which the bone bonds to the implant
3. **Second-stage surgery and healing abutment placement** — A minor procedure to expose the top of the implant (if it was submerged) and attach the healing abutment
4. **Soft tissue healing** — 2 to 4 weeks for the gum to heal and contour around the abutment
5. **Final restoration** — Impressions are taken, and the definitive abutment and crown are fabricated and placed

Some implant systems use a one-stage surgical approach in which the healing abutment is placed at the same time as the implant post, eliminating the need for a second procedure.

## What the Healing Abutment Does

When the implant post has been submerged beneath the gum for the osseointegration period, the surrounding gum tissue closes over it. The healing abutment, when placed, penetrates through the gum and creates an opening that:

- Allows the gum tissue to heal in a shape that matches the eventual crown profile
- Prevents gum tissue from growing over the implant top
- Creates the emergence profile — the contour at which the crown appears to emerge from the gum line

The shape and diameter of the healing abutment is selected to approximate the dimensions of the planned final crown.

## What to Expect During Healing Abutment Placement

For submerged implants, the second-stage procedure to place the healing abutment is generally minor:

- Local anesthesia is used
- A small incision or tissue punch is made to expose the implant top
- The healing abutment is threaded into place
- No sutures are typically required

Discomfort after this procedure is usually mild and short-lived.

## During the Healing Abutment Phase

While the healing abutment is in place:

- Gum tissue typically heals and contours within 2 to 4 weeks
- The abutment will be visible as a small metallic or white protrusion above the gum line
- Cleaning around the abutment with a soft brush and gentle water pressure is important
- Contact your provider if the abutment appears loose, comes out, or the surrounding tissue becomes significantly inflamed

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

- Will my treatment use a one-stage or two-stage implant protocol?
- If two-stage, when will the healing abutment be placed?
- How long will the healing abutment remain in place before the final impression?
- What does the healing abutment phase look like, and what will I see in the mirror?
