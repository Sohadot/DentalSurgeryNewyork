## Overview

Diet after oral surgery is one of the most common post-operative questions and one of the most practically important. What a patient eats — and avoids — in the days following surgery can affect bleeding, clot stability, healing speed, and the risk of complications such as dry socket.

This page covers the general principles that apply across most oral surgery procedures, along with the specific considerations for extraction and implant procedures.

## The First 24 Hours

The first day after most oral surgery procedures calls for the most caution:

- **Temperature:** Cold or room-temperature foods are generally preferred. Heat can increase bleeding and swelling.
- **Texture:** Soft foods only. Nothing that requires significant chewing or biting.
- **Pressure:** Avoid using a straw. The suction created by straw use can dislodge the blood clot in an extraction socket, contributing to dry socket.
- **Alcohol and smoking:** Both should be avoided. Alcohol can interact with pain medications and impair healing. Smoking significantly increases dry socket risk and impairs healing across all procedures.

Foods that are generally appropriate in the first 24 hours include:

- smoothies (consumed without a straw)
- yogurt
- applesauce
- broth or very soft soup (not hot)
- mashed potatoes
- ice cream or pudding

## Days 2 Through 7

As initial healing progresses, slightly more variety is typically tolerable, though still avoiding anything hard, crunchy, or chewy:

- scrambled eggs
- soft pasta
- soft-cooked vegetables
- fish
- oatmeal
- soft bread without crust

Avoid small particulate foods that can lodge in the socket: seeds, rice, nuts, popcorn. These can disrupt healing and cause irritation or infection.

## After Implant Procedures

Following implant placement specifically, the healing period is longer. While the first week follows similar soft-diet principles, patients should understand that hard or crunchy foods over the implant site should be avoided for the duration of the osseointegration period — often several months.

Providers typically give specific guidance about when to resume normal chewing function on the implant side. Patients should follow this guidance and ask for clarification if a timeline is not provided.

## What to Ask Before the Procedure

- Are there specific foods I should avoid given the type of procedure I’m having?
- How long should I follow a soft diet?
- Are there foods that are especially important to avoid in the first 48 hours?
- When can I resume normal eating?

## Provider Instructions Take Precedence

The guidance on this page reflects general principles. Individual providers may give specific dietary instructions based on the patient’s procedure, complexity, or health status. Provider-specific instructions should be followed over general guidance.
