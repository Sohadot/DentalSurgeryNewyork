## What Is Peri-Implant Mucositis?

Peri-implant mucositis is inflammation of the soft tissue — the gum — immediately surrounding a dental implant. It is the implant equivalent of gingivitis: a reversible condition caused by bacterial plaque accumulation at the implant-gum interface.

The clinical significance of peri-implant mucositis is that it is a warning sign. When addressed early with professional cleaning and improved home hygiene, the inflammation resolves without bone loss. When left unaddressed, it can progress to peri-implantitis — a more serious condition involving bone loss around the implant that is not fully reversible.

## How It Differs from Peri-Implantitis

The distinction is defined by what tissue is affected:

- **Peri-implant mucositis** — Inflammation confined to the soft tissue (gum). No bone loss. Reversible.
- **Peri-implantitis** — Inflammation extending into the bone supporting the implant. Bone loss is present and progressive if untreated. Not fully reversible.

Both conditions share the same origin: bacterial biofilm at the implant surface. The difference is how far the inflammatory process has extended.

## Signs of Peri-Implant Mucositis

Common signs include:

- Bleeding when the area is probed or brushed
- Redness or swelling of the gum tissue around the implant
- Tenderness at the implant site
- Slight pus or discharge at the gum margin in more advanced cases

Importantly, peri-implant mucositis is often painless in early stages. The absence of pain does not indicate the absence of a problem.

## Who Is at Risk

Risk factors for developing peri-implant mucositis include:

- Inadequate oral hygiene around the implant and adjacent teeth
- History of periodontal disease
- Smoking, which reduces the immune response in gum tissue
- Poorly fitting restorations that create plaque-trapping margins
- Infrequent professional maintenance after implant placement

Patients who had gum disease before implant placement are at elevated risk and should maintain a more frequent professional maintenance schedule.

## Treatment

Treatment for peri-implant mucositis typically involves:

- Professional debridement (cleaning) of the implant surface and surrounding tissue
- Patient education on implant-specific hygiene tools (interdental brushes, water flossers)
- Evaluation of the crown or abutment for any marginal fit issues

With consistent treatment and improved home care, peri-implant mucositis resolves in most cases. The condition should be documented and monitored at all recall visits.

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

Before an implant procedure:

- How will I know if I develop peri-implant mucositis after the implant is placed?
- How often should I return for implant-specific maintenance?
- What oral hygiene tools are recommended for around the implant?

If you already have implants:

- Has any inflammation been noted at my implant sites during this visit?
- Is probing depth increasing around any of my implants?
- What should I watch for between visits?
