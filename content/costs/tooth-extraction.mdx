## Introduction

Tooth extraction cost in New York depends primarily on whether the procedure is classified as simple or surgical. These two categories represent meaningfully different levels of clinical work, procedure time, and resources — and the distinction shapes every other element of the cost structure.

## Simple vs. Surgical Extraction: The Primary Cost Divide

A simple extraction involves a fully erupted tooth that can be removed with standard instruments, typically under local anesthesia. A surgical extraction involves a tooth that is impacted, broken at the gumline, has complex root anatomy, or requires incisions and bone removal.

In private practice settings in New York, simple extractions are commonly quoted in a range of roughly **$150 to $350 per tooth**. Surgical extractions — for impacted or complex cases — are frequently listed in a range of roughly **$300 to $800 or more per tooth**, before accounting for sedation, imaging, or follow-on procedures.

These are directional figures. They do not represent any specific provider's pricing.

## What Changes the Total Cost

Beyond whether the extraction is simple or surgical, total cost in New York may vary based on:

- **Imaging** — Some providers charge separately for X-rays or CBCT scans used to plan the procedure. Others include imaging in the quoted price.
- **Sedation or anesthesia** — Local anesthesia is standard, but nitrous oxide or IV sedation may be recommended depending on the case or patient anxiety. Each adds separately to total cost.
- **Emergency or same-day appointments** — Urgent extractions often carry additional fees.
- **Borough and practice setting** — Manhattan practices tend to have higher overhead reflected in pricing. Outer borough and community-based providers may present lower figures.
- **Follow-up care** — Some estimates include follow-up visits; others do not.

## Socket Preservation: A Common Add-On

Patients who may eventually want a dental implant should understand that socket preservation grafting — placing graft material into the extraction site at the time of removal — may be recommended as a separate procedure.

Socket preservation reduces post-extraction bone loss and may simplify future implant placement. It is billed separately and can add meaningfully to the cost of the extraction visit. Patients should ask about this option before extraction, not after.

For more on what socket preservation involves and costs, see the [Bone Grafting Cost in New York](/costs/bone-grafting) guide.

## What to Clarify Before Comparing Estimates

Before comparing providers, patients should request clarity on:

- whether the extraction is classified as simple or surgical
- whether imaging is included in the estimate
- whether sedation is included and at what level
- whether follow-up care or medications are included
- whether socket preservation is recommended and what it costs

A written itemized estimate makes these components visible and allows for a more useful comparison.
