## What Is Immediate Implant Placement?

Immediate implant placement refers to placing a dental implant into the socket on the same day a tooth is extracted, rather than waiting the traditional 3 to 6 months for the socket to heal before surgery. When it is appropriately indicated, it can reduce the total treatment timeline and preserve bone volume that would otherwise begin to resorb after extraction.

## How It Differs from the Standard Protocol

In the conventional (delayed) protocol:

1. Tooth is extracted
2. Socket is allowed to heal for 3 to 6 months
3. Implant post is placed
4. Osseointegration occurs over another 3 to 6 months
5. Crown is placed

In the immediate placement protocol:

1. Tooth is extracted
2. Implant post is placed in the same appointment
3. Osseointegration occurs over 3 to 6 months
4. Crown is placed

This eliminates one surgical appointment and one major waiting period — but it does not eliminate osseointegration time. An implant placed immediately is not functionally loaded immediately in most cases; the bone must still integrate before the final crown is placed.

## When Immediate Placement Is Appropriate

Immediate placement is not indicated for every extraction. Conditions that support immediate placement include:

- **Adequate bone volume** — The socket walls must be sufficiently intact to stabilize the implant at placement
- **Absence of active infection** — Acute periapical infection or uncontrolled periodontal disease at the extraction site is typically a contraindication
- **Favorable tooth anatomy** — Single-rooted teeth in the front of the mouth are more amenable than multi-rooted posterior teeth
- **Adequate primary stability** — The implant must achieve a minimum insertion torque at placement to begin osseointegration

When any of these conditions is not met, delayed placement is the safer approach.

## Immediate Loading vs. Immediate Placement

These terms are sometimes confused but are distinct:

- **Immediate placement** — Implant placed the same day as extraction
- **Immediate loading** (also called same-day teeth) — A temporary crown is placed on the implant at the same appointment, so the patient leaves with a tooth-shaped restoration

Immediate loading is only appropriate in specific cases where primary stability is very high and the opposing bite can be managed to minimize load on the implant during osseointegration. Not all immediate placements include immediate loading.

## Cost Implications

Immediate placement does not inherently reduce cost. In some cases it may reduce cost by eliminating a surgical appointment; in others, the complexity of the technique and the need for a temporary restoration during osseointegration may increase it. Patients should request a line-item estimate that distinguishes extraction cost from implant placement cost and includes the cost of any temporary restoration.

## Questions to Ask Before Agreeing to Immediate Placement

- Am I a candidate for immediate implant placement based on my specific case?
- What is the primary stability you expect to achieve at placement?
- Will I have a temporary crown, or will the site be left with a healing abutment?
- What happens if primary stability is not achieved at the time of extraction — will you delay placement?
- How does immediate placement compare in cost to the delayed protocol for my case?
