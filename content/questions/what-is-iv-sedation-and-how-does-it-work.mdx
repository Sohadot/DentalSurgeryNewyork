## Overview

IV sedation — intravenous sedation — is a form of moderate to deep sedation administered through a vein, typically in the arm. It is commonly used in oral surgery for procedures where local anesthesia alone would be insufficient for patient comfort, or where the procedure is complex and extended.

Understanding what IV sedation is, how it differs from other sedation types, and what to ask before agreeing to it is an important part of pre-treatment preparation.

## How IV Sedation Works

Sedative medications are delivered directly into the bloodstream through an intravenous line, producing a rapid and controllable level of sedation. Unlike oral sedation (a pill taken before the appointment), IV sedation allows the provider to:

- achieve a specific and adjustable level of sedation
- adjust the depth quickly if needed during the procedure
- administer reversal agents if necessary

Patients under IV sedation are typically in a state of deep relaxation or light sleep. Most have little to no memory of the procedure. However, they are generally not fully unconscious — this distinguishes IV sedation from general anesthesia, though the line between deep IV sedation and general anesthesia can be narrow depending on the medications and doses used.

## Who Administers IV Sedation

In New York, the administration of IV sedation for oral surgery requires specific state licensure beyond a general dentistry license. Oral and maxillofacial surgeons who have completed a hospital-based residency typically have general anesthesia permits that cover IV sedation. Other providers may have moderate sedation permits with more restricted scope.

Patients should ask:

- Who will administer the IV sedation?
- What permit or licensure does that person hold?
- Is the person who administers sedation the same person who performs the surgery, or is a separate sedation provider involved?
- What monitoring equipment is in place during the procedure?

## What to Expect Before, During, and After

**Before:** Patients must fast for a specified period — typically nothing to eat or drink (including water) for several hours before the appointment. Specific instructions vary by provider and protocol.

**During:** An IV line is placed, usually in the arm. Sedative medications are administered. Vital signs are monitored throughout the procedure.

**After:** Patients wake in the recovery area. A responsible adult must be present to drive the patient home — driving after IV sedation is not permitted. Residual drowsiness and impaired judgment can persist for the remainder of the day.

## How IV Sedation Affects Cost

IV sedation is typically billed separately from the surgical fee. The cost depends on the duration of sedation, the provider's fee structure, and whether the sedation is performed by the surgeon or a separate anesthesia provider. Patients should ask whether sedation is included in the quoted fee or billed as a separate line item.

## Questions to Ask Before Agreeing to IV Sedation

- Is IV sedation necessary for my procedure, or are lighter options available?
- What are the specific fasting requirements for my appointment?
- Who administers the sedation and what are their credentials?
- What monitoring is in place?
- Is the sedation fee separate from the surgical fee?
- What should I expect during recovery and how long will effects last?
