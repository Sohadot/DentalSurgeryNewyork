## Introduction

Patients who have been wearing conventional dentures for some time often ask whether dental implants are still an option. The answer is: frequently yes, but with important caveats. The primary challenge is bone loss — denture wearers typically experience significant resorption over time, and that bone loss directly affects implant candidacy and the treatment approach required. Understanding what the evaluation process looks like helps New York patients know what to expect.

---

## Why Bone Loss Is the Core Issue

Conventional dentures rest on the gum tissue. Unlike tooth roots or implants, they do not transmit functional load to the underlying bone. Without that stimulation, bone resorption continues after tooth loss — and in long-term denture wearers, it can be significant.

The degree of bone loss determines:
- Whether implants can be placed at all
- How many implants are feasible and where
- Whether bone grafting is required before implant placement
- Which prosthetic approach is appropriate

---

## What the Evaluation Looks Like

The evaluation for dental implants after dentures begins with:

1. **CBCT cone beam imaging** — A 3D scan of the jaw showing bone height, width, and density at potential implant sites, as well as proximity to nerves and sinus structures
2. **Bone volume assessment** — The oral surgeon measures available bone at planned implant locations and determines whether the bone is sufficient for standard implants, whether grafting is needed, or whether angled implant placement (as in All-on-4) can work around areas of deficiency
3. **Medical history review** — Systemic conditions affecting healing, current medications (especially bisphosphonates), and smoking history are evaluated

---

## What the Options Look Like

Depending on bone volume and patient goals, several approaches may be discussed:

**Implant-supported overdenture (2–4 implants)** — The existing or new denture attaches to implants via clips or bar connectors. The patient still removes the denture for cleaning. Lower cost and bone requirements than fixed options. Often achievable with 2 implants in the lower jaw.

**All-on-4 fixed full arch** — Four implants per arch support a fixed (non-removable) prosthesis. Requires more bone and more surgery, but produces a fixed result without daily denture removal. The angled back implants are specifically designed to work with reduced posterior bone volume.

**Bone grafting followed by implant placement** — If bone volume is significantly deficient, grafting (ridge augmentation, sinus lift, or both) may be needed before implants are placed. This adds time and cost but can restore bone sufficient for implant candidacy in some cases.

---

## What to Ask at the Consultation

- How much bone loss has occurred based on the scan, and how does that affect my options?
- Am I a candidate for the type of implant solution I’m considering, or are there limitations based on bone volume?
- If grafting is needed, what is the timeline and cost before implant placement is possible?
- What does the full treatment episode cost, from evaluation through final prosthesis?
