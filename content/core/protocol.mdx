## Introduction

Navigating oral surgery in New York involves a sequence of decisions spread across weeks or months. Most patients encounter these decisions without having anticipated what comes next. This protocol maps the typical patient journey through oral surgery in New York, identifying where decisions arise and what to clarify at each stage.

This is an informational framework. Specific timing and requirements vary by procedure, provider, and individual case.

---

## Phase 1: Recognition and Initial Assessment

### Trigger Events

The oral surgery process typically begins with one of the following:

- A referral from a general dentist for specialist evaluation
- A presenting symptom — pain, swelling, injury, or a tooth that cannot be saved
- An elective decision, such as replacing a missing tooth
- An emergency situation, such as acute infection or a broken tooth

### What Happens in Phase 1

- A primary care dentist or emergency provider performs an initial assessment
- Imaging is taken — typically an X-ray or CBCT cone beam scan
- A referral to an oral surgeon is issued, or a specialist within the same practice is recommended
- The patient receives a preliminary explanation of the presenting issue and a provisional treatment direction

### What to Clarify Before Leaving Phase 1

- Is a specialist referral required, or is this within a general dentist's scope?
- Who interprets the imaging, and who retains the imaging records?
- What is the urgency — is there time for comparison and second opinions?

---

## Phase 2: Specialist Consultation

### What Happens in Phase 2

- The patient meets with an oral surgeon (OMFS) or dental specialist
- Additional imaging may be ordered if not already available
- A diagnosis is confirmed and a treatment plan is proposed
- A written estimate or treatment planning document is provided

### Decision Points in Phase 2

Phase 2 is the highest-leverage point for patient decision-making. Most patients who receive one opinion do not seek a second. Key decision points include:

- Is the proposed treatment the only path, or are alternatives available?
- What does the estimate include — and what is explicitly excluded?
- What type of sedation is proposed, and who administers it?
- Are there staging options — such as extraction now and grafting later versus simultaneous procedures?

### Protocol Standard at Phase 2

Always request a written, itemized estimate before leaving the consultation. Verbal cost statements are not binding. A complete estimate should itemize: procedure code and fee, facility fee, imaging, anesthesia or sedation, and follow-up care.

For guidance on reading estimates, see: [Understanding Your Oral Surgery Estimate](/guides/understanding-your-oral-surgery-estimate)

---

## Phase 3: Pre-Authorization and Verification

### What Happens in Phase 3

If insurance coverage applies, the provider may submit a predetermination request to the insurer, which returns a coverage estimate before treatment begins. If no insurance is involved, payment terms are confirmed in writing.

### What to Clarify in Phase 3

- Has a predetermination been submitted, or is the estimate based on assumptions about typical coverage?
- What is the difference between the predetermination response and actual payment — and who absorbs the gap?
- Will sedation be billed separately, and does coverage apply to that component?
- What happens to the cost structure if additional work is needed during the procedure?

For insurance context, see: [Does Insurance Cover Oral Surgery in New York?](/questions/does-insurance-cover-oral-surgery-in-new-york)

---

## Phase 4: Pre-Operative Preparation

### What Happens in Phase 4

- Medical clearance may be required — especially for patients with systemic conditions or planned general anesthesia
- Pre-medications may be prescribed — antibiotics, anti-inflammatories
- NPO (nothing by mouth) instructions are issued for sedation cases
- Transportation arrangements are confirmed for patients receiving anything beyond local anesthesia
- Final consent is reviewed and signed

### Protocol Deviations to Watch For

- Consent forms presented at the last moment without time for review
- NPO instructions that conflict with prescription medication timing
- No written post-operative care instructions provided in advance
- Transportation or escort requirements not communicated until the day of the procedure

---

## Phase 5: Day of Procedure

For a structured walkthrough of the day of surgery, see: [What to Expect on the Day of Oral Surgery](/questions/what-to-expect-on-the-day-of-oral-surgery)

Key Phase 5 considerations:

- Any changes to the treatment plan proposed after arrival but before sedation require written acknowledgment
- Post-operative prescription instructions should be provided before sedation is administered
- A responsible adult should be confirmed to receive discharge instructions, as sedated patients may not retain verbal information

---

## Phase 6: Recovery and Follow-Up

### What Happens in Phase 6

- Initial recovery takes place in a supervised on-site recovery area
- Discharge occurs when vitals are stable and the escort is confirmed
- The patient follows written post-operative instructions at home
- A follow-up appointment is scheduled — timing depends on procedure and complexity
- Suture removal, imaging review, or next-stage planning occurs at follow-up

### What to Clarify Before Leaving Phase 5

- Who is the after-hours contact if unexpected symptoms arise?
- Which symptoms are urgent versus expected and manageable at home?
- What is the timeline for the follow-up appointment?
- If bone grafting or socket preservation was performed, what is the integration timeline before next steps?

---

## Phase 7: Subsequent Treatment or Monitoring

Some oral surgery procedures conclude at Phase 6. Others initiate a multi-phase sequence:

- **Extraction without grafting** — Phase 7 may involve monitoring and eventual implant consideration, or no further treatment
- **Socket preservation** — Integration period typically 3–6 months, then implant eligibility assessment
- **Bone graft** — Integration monitoring, then next-stage planning
- **Implant placement** — Osseointegration period, then abutment and crown placement

Each Phase 7 pathway has its own timeline and decision checkpoints. The protocol restarts from Phase 2 for each subsequent stage.

---

## Protocol Checkpoints Summary

| Phase | Purpose | Minimum Standard |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| Phase 1 | Understand the presenting issue | Written referral, imaging records |
| Phase 2 | Evaluate treatment proposal | Written itemized estimate |
| Phase 3 | Confirm insurance terms | Predetermination response in writing |
| Phase 4 | Prepare for the procedure | Written pre-op and post-op instructions |
| Phase 5 | Day of surgery | Reviewed and signed consent form |
| Phase 6 | Recovery and follow-up | After-hours contact, follow-up timeline |
| Phase 7 | Next-stage planning | Integration timeline documented |
