## Overview

A CBCT scan — cone beam computed tomography — is a specialized three-dimensional imaging technique used in oral surgery and dental implant planning. It provides significantly more information than a standard dental X-ray and is used to assess bone volume, anatomy, and proximity to nerves before certain procedures.

Understanding what a CBCT scan is, when it is used, and how it affects the cost and planning of treatment helps patients ask better questions before agreeing to imaging.

## What a CBCT Scan Shows

Unlike a standard periapical X-ray, which shows a two-dimensional image of individual teeth, or a panoramic X-ray, which gives a flat overview of the jaw, a CBCT scan creates a three-dimensional volumetric image. This allows the provider to:

- measure bone height, width, and density at a specific implant site
- visualize the position of the inferior alveolar nerve in the lower jaw
- assess sinus anatomy before upper jaw procedures
- identify bone defects or pathology not visible on standard X-rays
- plan implant placement with precise location and angulation

## When a CBCT Scan Is Typically Ordered

CBCT scans are commonly used when:

- dental implant placement is being planned, particularly in anatomically complex areas
- a sinus lift is being considered (to assess sinus floor height and health)
- bone grafting needs to be staged and the extent of bone loss needs to be quantified
- wisdom tooth removal involves roots near the inferior alveolar nerve
- pathology such as cysts or tumors needs three-dimensional assessment
- full mouth reconstruction is being planned and multiple sites require evaluation

Not every implant or extraction case requires a CBCT. Some straightforward single-tooth implants in uncomplicated anatomy may be planned from a panoramic X-ray and periapical films.

## How It Affects Cost

CBCT scans are an imaging fee separate from the surgical fee. Patients should ask:

- Is a CBCT scan included in the quoted fee, or is it billed separately?
- How is the scan interpreted and by whom?
- Will the scan be shared with other providers involved in treatment?

In some practices, CBCT is performed in-office. In others, patients are referred to a radiology facility. The cost structure differs depending on this arrangement.

## Radiation Considerations

CBCT imaging involves more radiation exposure than conventional dental X-rays but significantly less than a medical CT scan. The indication — whether the diagnostic benefit justifies the imaging — should be part of the conversation when a scan is recommended.

## Questions to Ask Before Imaging

- Why is a CBCT scan needed for my specific case?
- Is this imaging included in the quoted fee or billed separately?
- Will the scan findings change the treatment plan?
- Who will interpret the scan and communicate the findings?
