## Overview

Preparation for wisdom tooth removal depends on several variables: the complexity of the extraction, what type of sedation is planned, and what the provider has specified based on the individual case. This page describes the categories of preparation that typically apply and the questions worth asking before the procedure.

## Understanding What Is Planned

Before arriving for wisdom tooth removal, patients should understand:

- whether one or multiple teeth are being removed
- whether the extractions are simple or surgical (impacted)
- what type of sedation is planned
- who will be performing the procedure

This matters because preparation instructions differ significantly based on whether IV sedation or general anesthesia is involved versus local anesthesia only.

## If Sedation Is Involved

For procedures involving IV sedation or general anesthesia, preparation typically includes:

**Fasting requirements.** Most providers require patients to avoid food and drink — including water — for a specified period before the appointment. The fasting window varies by protocol and sedation type. Patients should follow the provider's specific instructions and ask about this explicitly if it is not covered.

**Medication considerations.** Patients should inform the provider of all medications, supplements, and herbal products they are taking. Some may need to be paused before surgery. This conversation should happen before the day of the procedure, not at check-in.

**Arranging transportation.** Any procedure involving IV sedation requires a responsible adult to drive the patient home. Patients cannot legally drive themselves after IV sedation, and many providers will not proceed without confirmation that transport is arranged.

**Clothing and personal items.** Loose, comfortable clothing is typically recommended. Removing nail polish and avoiding perfume or cologne before procedures involving general anesthesia may be requested. A photo ID and insurance card should be available.

## If Only Local Anesthesia Is Used

For simple extractions under local anesthesia only:

- Fasting requirements may be minimal or absent, but patients should ask
- Driving home may be possible, but some patients prefer not to
- Eating a light meal beforehand is generally acceptable
- Recovery time afterward is typically shorter

## Planning for Recovery

Preparation also includes planning what comes after:

- Arrange time away from work or school as needed, depending on complexity
- Stock appropriate foods in advance: soft foods, cold items for swelling management
- Understand what medications may be prescribed and whether they need to be filled before the appointment
- Identify the contact to reach in case of post-operative concerns

## Questions to Ask Before the Day

- What are the specific fasting instructions for my procedure?
- What medications should I stop or continue taking before the appointment?
- Do I need someone to drive me?
- What should I eat and avoid in the days after?
- Who should I contact if something seems wrong during recovery?
- Will I receive a prescription before the appointment or on the day?
