## Introduction

A sinus lift is a bone grafting procedure that adds bone volume to the upper jaw in the area of the back molars — where the maxillary sinuses are located. It is performed when there is not enough bone height in the upper jaw to place a dental implant securely without encroaching into the sinus cavity.

## Why Upper Jaw Bone Volume Matters for Implants

The upper back jaw presents a particular anatomical challenge for dental implants because the maxillary sinuses — air-filled spaces in the skull — sit directly above the bone that would receive the implant. In many patients, the combination of natural bone anatomy and the bone resorption that follows tooth loss results in insufficient vertical bone height between the jaw ridge and the sinus floor to safely place an implant of adequate length.

A sinus lift procedure addresses this by elevating the sinus membrane and placing bone graft material in the space created, increasing the bone height available for implant placement.

## Who May Need a Sinus Lift

Whether a sinus lift is needed is determined through clinical examination and imaging, typically including cone beam CT imaging that shows the three-dimensional anatomy of the jaw and sinus. Patients in the following situations may be more likely to require this procedure:

- Tooth loss in the upper back jaw, particularly the upper molars
- Significant bone resorption following extraction in that area
- Naturally thin bone in the posterior upper jaw
- History of extensive tooth loss in the upper jaw

A sinus lift is not required for all upper jaw implants. Implants in the front of the upper jaw, or in areas with adequate bone height, may not require any sinus augmentation.

## The Assessment Process

The need for a sinus lift cannot be determined by visual examination alone. Adequate imaging is required to measure the available bone height and evaluate the position and anatomy of the sinuses. This is why imaging — and specifically cone beam CT where appropriate — is part of thorough implant treatment planning.

Patients should ask whether this assessment has been performed and what findings support the treatment plan being recommended.

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

- Has imaging been done to assess my sinus anatomy and available bone height at this site?
- What is my current bone height at the planned implant site, and what height is needed?
- Is a sinus lift required, or are there alternative approaches to implant placement at this site?
- What does the sinus lift procedure involve, and how does it affect the overall treatment timeline?
- What additional cost does a sinus lift add to the treatment estimate?
