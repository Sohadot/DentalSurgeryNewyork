Full-arch dental implant treatment — replacing all teeth in the upper jaw, lower jaw, or both — is priced differently from single-tooth implants. Instead of a per-implant fee, providers quote a per-arch price that covers a defined set of components and services for the entire arch as a unit.

## What Per-Arch Pricing Typically Covers

A per-arch implant quote generally includes:

- **Implant posts** — The titanium fixtures placed in the jawbone. Full-arch cases use four to six implants per arch depending on the technique and bone volume.
- **Abutments** — The connector components between implant posts and the prosthetic arch.
- **Temporary prosthesis** — In immediate loading protocols (All-on-4, All-on-6), a temporary fixed arch is placed on the day of surgery, restoring function and appearance immediately while osseointegration proceeds.
- **Final prosthesis** — Delivered after osseointegration is confirmed, typically three to six months later. Material and fabrication complexity are the primary cost variables at this stage.
- **Surgical fees and anesthesia** — Included in most per-arch fees for full-arch cases.

## Common Exclusions

Even within a comprehensive per-arch quote, the following are frequently billed separately and can significantly affect total cost:

- CBCT scan (three-dimensional imaging for implant planning)
- Extractions of remaining teeth not already missing at the time of treatment
- Bone grafting if jaw volume does not support direct implant placement
- Pre-surgical medical evaluations or clearances
- Post-treatment maintenance visits beyond a defined warranty period

Request a written itemized breakdown — what is specifically included and what is a potential add-on — before accepting any per-arch quote.

## All-on-4 vs. Traditional Multi-Implant Arch Pricing

**All-on-4 (or All-on-6)** is a technique that supports a fixed arch prosthesis on four or six implants rather than individual implants for each missing tooth. It is typically quoted as a single per-arch fee. In New York, All-on-4 treatment with a final zirconia arch prosthesis ranges from approximately $25,000 to $45,000 or more per arch, depending on the provider, the implant system, and material specifications. This range can vary substantially between a private oral surgery practice and a dental service organization (DSO).

**Traditional per-tooth implants** for a full arch involve individual implant placements, abutments, and crowns for each missing tooth. For a full arch of eight to twelve implants with individual restorations, total treatment costs can exceed $60,000–$90,000 per arch depending on the number of implants and restoration type.

The per-arch All-on-4 model consolidates billing, which makes surface comparison easier but can obscure what phase-specific costs are actually included. Confirm whether the quoted price covers the temporary immediate prosthesis and the final arch, or only one of the two.

## Insurance and Per-Arch Treatment

Most dental insurance plans exclude implant-based full-arch treatment. Medical insurance billing is possible in documented cases involving trauma, congenital conditions, or specific medical necessity criteria — confirm with the provider whether medical billing will be attempted and what documentation is required. Flexible spending accounts (FSAs), health savings accounts (HSAs), and third-party healthcare credit products are commonly used to finance out-of-pocket costs in full-arch implant cases.

## What to Request Before Committing

Before accepting a full-arch treatment plan, request in writing:

1. The per-arch price with a specific list of what is included
2. Separate line-item pricing for likely add-ons: extractions, bone grafting, imaging
3. Material specifications for both the temporary and final prosthesis (acrylic, zirconia hybrid, monolithic zirconia)
4. A treatment timeline with defined phases and payment schedule

Full-arch implant treatment is among the largest single purchases patients make in oral surgery. Clarity on what the quoted number includes — before treatment begins — is directly proportional to avoiding mid-treatment cost disputes.
