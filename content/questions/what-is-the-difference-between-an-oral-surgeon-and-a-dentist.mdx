## Introduction

Not all dental providers who perform oral surgery procedures have the same level of surgical training. Understanding the difference between an oral and maxillofacial surgeon and a general dentist helps patients ask more informed questions when a procedure is recommended.

## What an Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeon Is

An oral and maxillofacial surgeon, commonly abbreviated as OMFS or OMS, is a dental specialist who has completed dental school followed by a hospital-based surgical residency program. That residency typically spans four to six years and includes advanced training in surgical procedures involving the mouth, jaw, and related structures, as well as training in anesthesia and sedation administration.

In New York, providers who administer IV sedation or general anesthesia in outpatient settings are subject to specific permitting requirements from the New York State Education Department. Oral surgeons holding these permits have demonstrated compliance with training and facility standards beyond general dental licensure.

## What a General Dentist Can and Cannot Do

A general dentist may perform certain oral surgery procedures, including routine tooth extractions, depending on the complexity of the case and their individual training. However, many procedures involve levels of surgical complexity, impaction, sedation requirements, or anatomical considerations that exceed the typical scope of general dental practice.

Some procedures — including complex impaction removal, dental implant placement in surgically challenging cases, bone grafting, jaw-related surgery, and cases requiring IV sedation or general anesthesia — are more commonly handled by a board-eligible or board-certified oral surgeon.

## When Specialist Involvement Tends to Matter

Specialist involvement is more commonly expected when:

- the extraction involves deep impaction or an unusual root anatomy
- bone grafting is part of the planned treatment
- implant placement requires complex surgical planning or staging
- IV sedation or general anesthesia will be administered
- the case involves structures beyond the tooth itself

## How to Verify Provider Credentials

Patients can ask directly:

- Is the provider performing this procedure a licensed oral and maxillofacial surgeon?
- Where did the provider complete their surgical residency?
- Does the provider hold a current anesthesia permit for the sedation level being recommended?
- Is the facility where treatment will occur equipped and permitted for the planned procedure?
