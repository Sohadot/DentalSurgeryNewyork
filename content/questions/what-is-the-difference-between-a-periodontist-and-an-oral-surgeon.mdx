## Introduction

Patients planning implants, bone grafting, or complex extractions in New York may receive referrals to either a periodontist or an oral surgeon — or encounter both specialists. The two disciplines overlap in several areas, particularly implant placement, but their training pathways, credential structures, and scope of practice differ. Understanding those differences helps patients ask more precise questions before choosing a specialist.

---

## What an Oral Maxillofacial Surgeon (OMFS) Does

An oral and maxillofacial surgeon completes a dental degree (DDS or DMD) followed by a 4–6 year hospital-based surgical residency. That residency includes general surgery rotations, anesthesia training, and management of facial injuries, infections, tumors, and jaw disorders alongside dental surgical procedures.

In New York, oral surgeons are licensed to:

- Perform tooth extractions, including impacted wisdom teeth
- Place dental implants
- Perform bone grafting procedures — socket preservation, ridge augmentation, sinus lift
- Administer general anesthesia and deep sedation in an office setting
- Treat facial fractures, jaw joint disorders, and soft tissue pathology
- Manage oral infections and facial emergencies

The OMFS credential is the relevant one for complex extractions, facial injuries, infections with systemic involvement, and cases requiring IV or general anesthesia.

---

## What a Periodontist Does

A periodontist completes a dental degree followed by a 3-year accredited postgraduate program in periodontics — the specialty focused on gum tissue, the supporting bone structures of teeth, and the management of periodontal disease.

In New York, periodontists are licensed to:

- Diagnose and treat gum disease at all stages
- Perform gum surgery — recession treatment, bone regeneration around teeth
- Place dental implants
- Perform bone grafting procedures around existing teeth and implant sites

The periodontist credential is the relevant one for active gum disease management, gum recession, and cases where the surrounding tooth structures require ongoing specialist care.

---

## Where the Scopes Overlap: Dental Implants

Both oral surgeons and periodontists place dental implants in New York. Neither automatically produces better outcomes — case complexity, provider experience, and individual anatomy matter more than specialty label in straightforward implant cases.

The distinction becomes more relevant when:

- **Significant bone grafting is required** — Oral surgeons with formal surgical training typically manage complex grafting, sinus lifts, and cases requiring IV sedation or general anesthesia
- **Active gum disease is present** — Periodontal disease affecting the implant site should be resolved before placement; a periodontist may be the appropriate first specialist
- **Medical complexity is involved** — Cases with systemic conditions that may require hospital-based care fall within OMFS scope
- **Both tissue and bone need management** — Some complex cases involve both a periodontist and an oral surgeon at different stages

---

## Questions to Ask When Choosing Between Specialists

Before selecting a specialist in New York, consider asking:

- Is the primary issue gum tissue and periodontal health, or surgical bone and jaw structure?
- Does the treatment plan involve IV sedation or general anesthesia — and who is credentialed to administer it?
- Is the recommended provider board-certified in their specialty?
- If both a periodontist and an oral surgeon have been recommended, what specifically does each provider handle, and why are both involved?
