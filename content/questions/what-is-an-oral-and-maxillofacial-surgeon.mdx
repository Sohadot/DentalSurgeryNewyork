## Overview

An oral and maxillofacial surgeon (OMS or OMFS) is a dental specialist whose scope of practice extends beyond general dentistry to include surgical procedures involving the mouth, jaws, face, and associated structures. Understanding what an oral and maxillofacial surgeon is, how the training differs from general dentistry, and when their involvement is relevant helps patients ask more informed questions before treatment.

## Training and Credentials

Oral and maxillofacial surgeons complete dental school followed by a hospital-based surgical residency program that is typically four to six years in length. This residency involves training in:

- dentoalveolar surgery (tooth extractions, impacted teeth)
- dental implant placement
- bone grafting and reconstructive procedures
- corrective jaw surgery (orthognathic surgery)
- facial trauma and reconstruction
- pathology (cysts, tumors) of the oral and maxillofacial region
- anesthesia administration, including IV sedation and general anesthesia

Board certification through the American Board of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery (ABOMS) is available and signifies that the surgeon has met additional examination requirements. Board certification is not required to practice but is a signal worth asking about.

## Scope of Practice vs. General Dentistry

General dentists can perform many common dental procedures, including simple tooth extractions. However, procedures that require:

- IV sedation or general anesthesia
- surgical access to bone
- complex impacted tooth removal
- bone grafting
- implant placement in complex cases
- jaw surgery or facial trauma management

...are typically within the scope of an oral and maxillofacial surgeon rather than a general dentist.

In New York, the authority to administer IV sedation and general anesthesia requires specific state permits beyond a general dentistry license. OMS residency training typically includes this qualification.

## How OMFS Differs From a Periodontist

Periodontists are also dental specialists who may perform procedures including dental implant placement and bone grafting. Their training focuses specifically on the periodontium — the structures supporting the teeth, including gum tissue and bone. OMS training has a broader surgical scope that includes jaw and facial structures.

Both specialists may be appropriate for certain procedures. The relevant question is which specialist is trained and credentialed for the specific procedure being considered.

## When a Patient Is Referred to an OMFS

Patients are typically referred to an oral and maxillofacial surgeon when:

- tooth removal is complex (deeply impacted, surgically involved)
- bone grafting is needed before or alongside implant placement
- IV sedation or general anesthesia is required or preferred
- pathology such as a cyst or tumor requires surgical evaluation
- a prior procedure failed or requires revision
- jaw surgery is being considered

## Questions to Ask

- Are you a board-certified oral and maxillofacial surgeon?
- What training and credentials are relevant to the procedure I need?
- Do you hold an IV sedation or general anesthesia permit in New York?
- Is this procedure within your specific scope of practice, and why are you recommending this approach?
