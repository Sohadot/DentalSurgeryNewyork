## How Long Does a Tooth Extraction Take?

Tooth extraction duration depends primarily on the complexity of the procedure — whether it is a simple extraction or a surgical one, and how the tooth is positioned in the jaw.

## Simple Extractions

A simple extraction involves a tooth that has fully erupted through the gumline, is intact, and has straightforward root anatomy. For a simple extraction, the time in the chair is typically **20 to 40 minutes**. The extraction itself — from instrument placement to tooth removal — usually takes 5 to 15 minutes. The rest of the appointment covers preparation, anesthesia administration, and post-extraction instructions.

## Surgical Extractions

Surgical extractions involve teeth that are partially erupted, impacted, broken at the gumline, or have curved or multiple roots that resist straightforward removal. These require incisions, bone removal, or sectioning the tooth before extraction. Surgical appointments typically run **45 to 90 minutes** depending on complexity.

## Wisdom Tooth Removal

Wisdom tooth removal duration varies widely. A single erupted wisdom tooth may take as little as 30 minutes total. A case involving four impacted wisdom teeth — especially lower third molars with deep positioning — can take 60 to 90 minutes or longer.

Key variables affecting duration:

- **Impaction depth** — Deeply buried teeth require more bone removal
- **Angulation** — Horizontal or mesioangular impactions are more complex than vertical ones
- **Root anatomy** — Fused roots remove more cleanly; curved or divergent roots complicate sectioning
- **Bone density** — Higher density bone requires more surgical time
- **Proximity to nerves** — Lower wisdom teeth near the inferior alveolar nerve require more conservative technique

## Same-Day Multiple Extractions

Some patients have multiple teeth removed in a single appointment. Surgeons schedule additional time for these cases. A multiple-extraction appointment can run 90 minutes to two hours including preparation and post-operative discussion.

## Recovery Time Versus Procedure Time

In-chair time is only part of the picture. Recovery from a simple extraction typically takes a few days. Recovery from a surgical extraction — particularly impacted wisdom teeth — usually takes one to two weeks before most patients return to normal activity. Bone healing continues for several months after that, particularly if the site is being preserved for a future implant.

## What to Ask Before Your Appointment

Before scheduling, ask the surgeon's office:

- Is this expected to be a simple or surgical extraction?
- How long should I block out for the appointment?
- If I am having sedation, how long before the appointment should I arrive?
- How long will I need to arrange for someone to drive me home?

For procedures involving IV sedation or general anesthesia, allow additional time before and after the procedure itself.
