## Can You Smoke After Tooth Extraction?

Smoking after tooth extraction significantly increases the risk of complications, particularly dry socket (alveolar osteitis) — a painful condition in which the blood clot that forms in the extraction socket is dislodged or fails to develop. Most providers recommend avoiding smoking for a minimum of **48 to 72 hours** after extraction, with longer abstinence strongly preferred.

## Why Smoking Increases Complication Risk

Two mechanisms make smoking particularly harmful in the post-extraction period:

### Suction and Clot Disruption
The act of drawing on a cigarette creates negative pressure in the mouth. This suction can physically dislodge the blood clot that forms in the extraction socket during the first 24 to 48 hours — the same mechanism that makes using straws inadvisable during this period. Without a stable clot, the bone and nerve endings in the socket are exposed, causing the sharp, persistent pain characteristic of dry socket.

### Nicotine and Vasoconstriction
Nicotine causes vasoconstriction — narrowing of blood vessels — which reduces blood flow to the healing tissue. Reduced circulation slows the delivery of oxygen and immune cells to the site, impairing clot formation and tissue repair even when the suction issue is avoided (as with nicotine patches or gum).

## Dry Socket Risk

Smokers have a significantly higher rate of dry socket after tooth extraction than non-smokers. Studies have reported dry socket rates of 12 percent or higher in smokers compared to 2 to 4 percent in non-smokers. The risk is highest in the first 48 to 72 hours after extraction, but remains elevated for up to a week.

Dry socket is not an infection, but it is painful and requires a return visit for treatment — typically irrigation and placement of a medicated dressing in the socket.

## Minimum Abstinence Guidance

Most oral surgeons recommend avoiding smoking for:

- **At minimum 48 to 72 hours** after a simple extraction
- **At minimum 5 to 7 days** after a surgical extraction or impacted wisdom tooth removal
- **Ideally 2 weeks or more** after bone grafting at the extraction site

The longer the abstinence, the better the healing outcome. This is especially true if the extraction site is intended as the future location for a dental implant.

## If You Smoke Regularly: What to Tell Your Surgeon

Disclosing smoking history to your oral surgeon before the procedure is important for two reasons:

1. It allows the surgeon to advise on realistic healing expectations and complication rates for your specific case
2. In some cases — particularly for implant patients — the surgeon may recommend a period of smoking cessation before the procedure, not just after

Nicotine replacement products (patches, gum, lozenges) avoid the suction mechanism but do not eliminate the vasoconstriction effect. They are preferable to smoking in the post-extraction period but are not without risk. Consult your provider.

## Signs of Dry Socket

If you develop dry socket, typical signs include:

- Throbbing pain beginning 2 to 4 days after extraction that is getting worse, not better
- Pain that radiates toward the ear, eye, temple, or neck on the same side
- Visible empty-looking socket (grey or white bone may be visible)
- Bad breath or unpleasant taste not explained by food

Contact your oral surgeon if you experience these symptoms. Dry socket is manageable but requires prompt attention.
