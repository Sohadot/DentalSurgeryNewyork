## Introduction

All-on-4 is a full-arch dental implant configuration designed to replace all teeth in an upper or lower jaw using four strategically placed implants. It is a distinct treatment approach from both traditional single-tooth implants and conventional removable dentures. Understanding the differences helps New York patients ask better questions before choosing a full-arch tooth replacement strategy.

---

## What All-on-4 Is

In a conventional dental implant approach, each missing tooth is replaced by its own implant post — a one-to-one relationship between implant and tooth. For patients missing all or most of their teeth, this would require 10–14 or more implants per arch, which is often impractical due to bone availability and cost.

All-on-4 uses four implants per arch to anchor a full set of fixed teeth. Two implants are placed vertically at the front of the jaw, and two are angled (typically at 45 degrees) toward the back to maximize engagement with available bone — often avoiding the areas where bone loss has been most significant.

The result is a fixed (non-removable) full-arch prosthesis supported by four implants, placed in a single surgical session with immediate temporary teeth in many cases.

---

## How It Differs from Traditional Single-Tooth Implants

| | Traditional Implants | All-on-4 |
|--|--|--|
| **Use case** | One to several missing teeth | Replacing an entire arch |
| **Implants required** | One per tooth | Four per arch |
| **Bone requirements** | Site-specific bone volume needed | Angled implants can work with reduced bone |
| **Timeline** | Sequential stages, months apart | Often immediate provisional teeth |
| **Restoration type** | Individual crowns | Fixed full-arch bridge |
| **Maintenance** | Individual teeth cleaned normally | Bridge requires specific hygiene tools |

---

## How It Differs from Conventional Dentures

Conventional complete dentures rest on the gum tissue and are removed for cleaning. They can shift during eating or speaking, do not transmit functional load to the jawbone, and require adhesive in many cases.

All-on-4 prostheses are fixed — they are screwed to the implants and are not removed by the patient. They feel and function more like natural teeth, transmit load to the jawbone (which helps preserve bone), and do not require adhesive.

Implant-supported overdentures ("snap-on" dentures) are a middle option: they attach to implants but are still removable by the patient. They typically use 2–4 implants per arch and are less expensive than a fully fixed All-on-4 system.

---

## When All-on-4 Is and Is Not Appropriate

All-on-4 is typically considered when:

- All or most teeth in an arch are missing or non-restorable
- The patient has enough remaining bone to support 4 implants (with or without angling)
- The patient is not a candidate for conventional dentures due to bone loss or tolerance
- The patient prefers a fixed restoration over a removable one

All-on-4 is typically not the appropriate choice when:

- Only a few teeth are missing (single or multiple traditional implants are more appropriate)
- Significant bone loss makes even angled implant placement insufficient (bone grafting may be needed first)
- The patient has active infection, severe periodontal disease, or systemic conditions that preclude implant surgery

---

## Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Full-Arch Solution

- Has a CBCT scan been performed to assess bone volume at all four planned implant sites?
- Is the proposed system truly an All-on-4 fixed arch, or an implant-supported overdenture — and what is the difference in my case?
- Will I have temporary teeth immediately, and if so, what restrictions apply during osseointegration?
- Who fabricates the final prosthesis, and what is the handoff between the surgeon and the prosthodontist?
- What does the full treatment episode cost — surgery, implants, temporary prosthesis, final prosthesis, and follow-up?
