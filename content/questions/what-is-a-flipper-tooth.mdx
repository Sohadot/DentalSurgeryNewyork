A flipper tooth is a removable partial denture used as a temporary tooth replacement. It consists of an acrylic base that fits against the gum and palate, with one or more prosthetic teeth attached. Small wire or acrylic clasps anchor it to adjacent teeth. The patient inserts and removes it themselves.

In oral surgery contexts, flippers are most commonly used to restore appearance during the months between a tooth extraction and the final crown placement on a dental implant — a period that typically spans three to six months under standard implant protocols.

## When a Flipper Is Appropriate

A flipper is most relevant for visible front teeth (incisors and canines) where leaving a gap during healing is aesthetically significant to the patient. For posterior molars, flippers are less commonly prescribed because the gap is generally not visible and the clasping pressure on adjacent teeth and the healing socket adds unnecessary complexity.

A flipper may not be appropriate if:
- Adjacent teeth are too compromised to support clasps
- The extraction site requires unobstructed healing without prosthetic pressure
- The patient has difficulty managing a removable appliance
- The oral surgeon prefers a period of uninterrupted socket healing

Your surgeon should confirm whether a flipper is compatible with your specific extraction and implant plan before one is fabricated.

## Cost in New York

A basic flipper partial in New York typically costs between $300 and $700, depending on the number of teeth replaced, the fabrication laboratory, and the provider. This is substantially lower than a fixed provisional bridge, which can cost $1,500–$3,000 or more for a single-tooth temporary replacement.

Some dental insurance plans provide partial coverage for interim partials. Confirm whether your plan classifies it as a partial denture and whether waiting periods apply before fabrication.

## Limitations

**Not a long-term solution.** Flippers are designed for temporary use. The acrylic base can wear, break, or fit less securely over time as healing changes the ridge contour beneath it. They are not intended for indefinite use.

**Dietary restrictions apply.** Hard, crunchy, or sticky foods should be avoided while wearing a flipper. Most surgeons recommend removing it during meals, particularly in the first weeks after extraction.

**Hygiene is essential.** The flipper must be cleaned daily and removed at night. Food and bacteria accumulate under the acrylic base if it is worn continuously without cleaning.

**Does not preserve bone.** A flipper maintains appearance during healing but does not prevent bone resorption at the extraction site. Socket preservation grafting, if indicated, should be addressed at the time of extraction — not deferred because a flipper is in place.

## Alternatives

For patients seeking a fixed temporary option, a Maryland bridge (a resin-bonded bridge using minimal preparation on adjacent teeth) provides a non-removable interim solution. It is more expensive than a flipper but does not require daily removal and offers more stable function during the implant healing period. The Maryland bridge is most practical for a single anterior tooth with healthy adjacent teeth.
