## What Is the Cost of Full-Mouth Reconstruction?

Full-mouth reconstruction is not a single procedure with a standard price. It is a multi-phase, multi-specialist treatment plan that addresses multiple missing, failing, or severely damaged teeth — and its cost reflects that complexity.

In New York City, full-mouth reconstruction typically ranges from **$30,000 to $90,000 or more**, depending on the number of teeth involved, what structural work is required before restoration can begin, the materials selected for final restorations, and the credentials and facility costs of the providers involved.

## What Drives the Cost

### Number of Implants

Each dental implant is an independent surgical and restorative event. A case involving six implants will cost significantly more than one involving two, and a full-arch case (All-on-4 or similar) is priced differently from a piecemeal approach. The per-implant cost in New York typically ranges from $3,500 to $6,500 before grafting, crowns, or ancillary procedures.

### Bone Grafting

Patients who have had missing teeth for an extended period commonly have bone loss that must be addressed before implants can be placed. Socket preservation, localized grafts, sinus lifts, and block grafts are priced separately. A single sinus lift in New York can add $2,500 to $5,000 per side.

### Existing Tooth Removal

If failing natural teeth need to be extracted before restoration begins, those extractions — particularly surgical ones — add to the total. Extraction costs range from a few hundred dollars for simple cases to over $1,000 per tooth for complex surgical extractions.

### Crown and Prosthetic Materials

Final restorations — crowns, bridges, or implant-supported overdentures — vary in material cost. Zirconia crowns carry a higher materials cost than porcelain-fused-to-metal alternatives. Full-arch implant bridges are priced as a unit, typically $15,000 to $30,000 per arch for the prosthetic component alone.

### Provider Credentials and Facility

Oral and maxillofacial surgeons, periodontists, prosthodontists, and general dentists with implant training may all be involved in a full-mouth reconstruction case. Hospital-based or accredited ambulatory surgical facility cases carry facility fees. Board-certified specialists at credentialed facilities typically charge at the higher end of the range.

## Insurance and Coverage

Dental insurance rarely covers more than a small fraction of full-mouth reconstruction costs. Some components may have partial coverage — extractions, for example, or a portion of a crown allowance — but aggregate coverage rarely exceeds $1,500 to $2,000 annually under most dental plans.

Patients should request a full itemized treatment plan with individual line-item costs before committing. This allows comparison across providers on equivalent terms and identifies which components are discretionary.

## How to Evaluate a Quote

A written estimate for full-mouth reconstruction should include:

- Itemized cost per extraction, graft, implant post, abutment, and crown
- Sedation costs (type, provider credentials, duration)
- Facility fees if applicable
- Anticipated staging and timing (phased treatment versus accelerated protocol)
- What is not included and what conditions could change the scope

Be cautious of lump-sum quotes that cannot be broken into components. The inability to itemize typically signals the inability to compare.
