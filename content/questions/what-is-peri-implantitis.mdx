## Overview

Peri-implantitis is an infection of the tissue and bone surrounding a dental implant. It is one of the most significant late-stage complications of dental implant treatment and is the primary cause of implant failure in patients who initially achieved successful osseointegration.

Understanding what peri-implantitis is, what causes it, and what questions to ask before and after treatment can help patients make more informed decisions.

## What Peri-Implantitis Is

Peri-implantitis is characterized by:

- inflammation of the soft tissue (gingiva) immediately surrounding the implant
- progressive bone loss around the implant fixture
- in advanced cases, mobility of the implant itself

It is analogous to periodontitis (gum disease) around natural teeth, but the tissue architecture around implants responds differently, which affects both susceptibility and treatment options.

Early-stage peri-implantitis may involve only soft tissue inflammation (called peri-implant mucositis) without bone loss. If not addressed, this can progress to the bone-destructive form.

## What Causes It

The primary cause is bacterial biofilm accumulation at the implant surface and in the space between the implant and surrounding tissue. Contributing factors include:

- history of periodontal disease (patients with a prior gum disease history have higher risk)
- smoking, which impairs immune response and tissue healing
- poorly controlled diabetes
- inadequate oral hygiene around the implant
- mechanical factors such as excess cement from the crown restoration
- implant positioning that makes cleaning difficult
- parafunctional habits (bruxism) that create excess occlusal stress

## How It Differs From Early Implant Failure

Early implant failure occurs during or shortly after the osseointegration period — typically within the first few months of placement. Peri-implantitis is a late complication, occurring months to years after the implant has successfully integrated.

The distinction matters for risk assessment: early failure is often related to surgical factors or systemic health conditions affecting bone healing. Peri-implantitis is primarily a maintenance and systemic risk factor issue.

## Signs That May Warrant Evaluation

Patients who have implants should be aware of:

- bleeding or discharge around the implant
- increasing discomfort or sensitivity around a previously comfortable implant
- visible gum recession around the implant
- any sense of looseness or movement

These signs should prompt contact with the treating provider, not a wait-and-see approach.

## Questions to Ask Before Implant Treatment

- Do I have any risk factors for peri-implantitis that should be addressed before treatment?
- What does long-term maintenance of the implant involve?
- What is the monitoring protocol for implant health after placement?
- If peri-implantitis develops, what are the treatment options?
