## Overview

Bone grafting in oral surgery is a procedure that rebuilds or augments jaw bone volume using graft material — allowing implant placement in areas where bone has been lost or where there is insufficient volume to safely anchor an implant post.

This page explains the mechanics of bone grafting, the types of graft material, and how the procedure fits into the broader implant treatment sequence.

## Why Bone Volume Matters for Implants

A dental implant post requires adequate bone height and width to be placed securely and to allow osseointegration. When bone has been lost — whether through tooth loss, infection, trauma, or resorption over time — the remaining volume may be insufficient for implant placement without preparatory grafting.

The relevant dimensions are:

- **Bone height:** how much vertical bone exists between the proposed implant site and structures below (the inferior alveolar nerve in the lower jaw) or above (the sinus floor in the upper jaw)
- **Bone width:** the horizontal dimension of the ridge at the proposed implant site

Insufficient height or width in either dimension may indicate that grafting is needed before implant placement.

## Types of Bone Grafting Procedures

### Socket Preservation

Also called alveolar ridge preservation, socket preservation is performed at the time of tooth extraction. Graft material is placed into the empty socket to slow or prevent the bone resorption that typically follows extraction. It preserves the dimensions of the ridge for potential future implant placement.

### Ridge Augmentation

Ridge augmentation is performed when the bone ridge has already narrowed or resorbed and needs to be widened or rebuilt before an implant can be placed. It involves placing graft material against the existing bone and protecting it with a membrane during the healing period.

### Sinus Lift

A sinus lift (sinus augmentation) is performed when the floor of the maxillary sinus sits too close to the proposed implant site in the upper jaw. Graft material is placed below the sinus membrane to build the bone height needed for implant placement. This can be performed as an internal (transcrestal) or external (lateral window) approach depending on how much height is needed.

## What Graft Material Is Used

Graft material comes from several sources:

- **Autograft:** bone taken from the patient’s own body (chin, ramus, hip). Generally considered the gold standard for integration but involves a second surgical site.
- **Allograft:** processed bone from a human donor. Widely used, no second surgical site required.
- **Xenograft:** bone material derived from animal sources (most commonly bovine). Also widely used.
- **Alloplast:** synthetic graft materials.

The choice depends on the size of the defect, the provider’s preferred protocol, and clinical factors. Patients can ask which material is being used and why.

## The Healing Timeline

Bone grafting adds time to the treatment sequence because the graft must mature before implant placement. Healing timelines vary by procedure:

- Socket preservation: typically 3–6 months before implant placement
- Ridge augmentation: typically 4–6 months
- Sinus lift (lateral window): typically 6–9 months

These timelines can extend the total implant treatment duration significantly.

## Questions to Ask Before Bone Grafting

- Why is grafting needed in my case and what type is recommended?
- What graft material will be used and why?
- How does this affect the timeline before implant placement?
- What is the cost of grafting and is it included in the implant quote?
- What happens if the graft does not integrate as expected?
