## What Does Board Certified Mean for Oral Surgeons?

Board certification for oral and maxillofacial surgeons is a voluntary credential issued by the **American Board of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery (ABOMS)**. It is separate from state licensure and not required to practice as an oral surgeon — but it represents a higher threshold of demonstrated competency than a license alone.

Understanding what board certification requires, and how to verify it, helps patients evaluate providers on objective criteria rather than marketing claims.

## How Oral Surgeons Become Board Certified

Board certification by ABOMS requires:

1. **Completion of an accredited residency** — A 4- to 6-year oral and maxillofacial surgery residency accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation (CODA)
2. **Written qualifying examination** — A multi-stage written exam covering the breadth of OMFS knowledge domains
3. **Oral clinical examination** — An in-person examination before a panel of examiners including case presentation and clinical reasoning
4. **Case documentation** — Submission of documented surgical cases demonstrating competency across procedure categories

Not every residency graduate pursues board certification. The process requires additional time and preparation beyond training completion, and it is voluntary.

## Maintenance of Certification

ABOMS board certification is not permanent. Certified surgeons must:

- Complete continuing education requirements
- Pass periodic re-examination (re-certification is required on a 10-year cycle)
- Maintain active OMFS practice

This maintenance requirement distinguishes active board certification from a historical credential that reflects only past training.

## Board Certification Versus State Licensure

State licensure — issued by the New York State Education Department — is the legal minimum required to practice oral and maxillofacial surgery in New York. Licensure requires graduation from an accredited dental school, completion of residency training, and passing board-approved licensing examinations.

Board certification by ABOMS is a separate, additional credential that goes beyond licensure requirements. A licensed oral surgeon without board certification is legally qualified to practice. A board-certified surgeon has met an additional voluntary standard of peer-assessed competency.

## Hospital Privileges

Separately from ABOMS certification, many oral surgeons hold hospital privileges — authorization to perform procedures at one or more hospitals. Hospital credentialing committees typically review training, case volume, outcome data, and professional references before granting privileges.

Hospital privileges are not the same as board certification, but they represent an additional layer of institutional review beyond state licensure.

## How to Verify Board Certification

ABOMS maintains a public verification tool where patients can confirm the current certification status of any oral and maxillofacial surgeon. Verification shows whether the surgeon is currently certified, when certification was issued, and whether it is current or lapsed.

Patients should verify certification directly rather than relying on a website's claim. **"Board eligible"** — a common marketing phrase — means the surgeon has completed training but has not yet passed the certification examinations. It is not equivalent to board certified.

## What to Ask Your Surgeon

- Are you currently board certified by the American Board of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery?
- Do you hold hospital privileges, and at which institutions?
- How many cases of this specific procedure have you performed in the past year?
