## Overview

Pericoronitis is an infection of the soft tissue surrounding a partially erupted tooth — most commonly a lower wisdom tooth. It is one of the most common reasons patients seek emergency dental or oral surgery care related to wisdom teeth, and it can range from mild discomfort to a more serious spreading infection requiring prompt treatment.

## What Causes Pericoronitis

Pericoronitis occurs when a wisdom tooth has partially emerged through the gum but remains partially covered by a flap of gum tissue called the operculum. This flap creates a pocket where:

- food and debris accumulate and are difficult to clean
- bacteria proliferate in the warm, moist environment
- the overlying tissue becomes inflamed and infected

Partially erupted teeth can sit in this state for months or years, making pericoronitis a recurring risk whenever the area becomes irritated or oral hygiene is disrupted.

## Symptoms

Common signs of pericoronitis include:

- pain or throbbing in the back of the mouth near the wisdom tooth
- swelling and redness of the surrounding gum tissue
- difficulty fully opening the mouth (trismus)
- an unpleasant taste or discharge from the area
- pain when biting or chewing that worsens with pressure on the area
- in more advanced cases, swelling in the cheek or jaw, difficulty swallowing, or fever

Mild pericoronitis may resolve with cleaning and irrigation of the area. Moderate to severe cases typically require antibiotics. Spreading infection or airway symptoms require urgent medical attention.

## When It Becomes a More Serious Concern

Pericoronitis that is not treated or that recurs repeatedly can spread beyond the gum tissue:

- Infection can spread into the surrounding facial spaces (submandibular, masseteric)
- In severe cases, spreading infection can become a medical emergency requiring hospitalization
- Repeated episodes increase the risk of damage to adjacent teeth

The severity signals that warrant urgent evaluation include:

- fever
- significant facial swelling extending beyond the jaw
- difficulty swallowing
- difficulty opening the mouth beyond a finger-width

## Relationship to Wisdom Tooth Removal

A history of pericoronitis is one of the most common indications for wisdom tooth removal in adults. If a partially erupted wisdom tooth has caused one or more episodes of pericoronitis, many providers will recommend removal to eliminate the recurring risk rather than continuing to manage episodes as they occur.

The timing of removal matters: most providers prefer to remove the tooth when the acute infection has resolved rather than during an active episode, though this depends on the clinical situation.

## Questions to Ask If You Have Symptoms

- Is this pericoronitis, and how severe is it?
- Does this require antibiotics?
- Should this wisdom tooth be removed, and if so, when?
- Are there signs of spreading infection I should watch for?
- What are the warning symptoms that would require urgent care?
