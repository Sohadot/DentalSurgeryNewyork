## Introduction

Dental implants have a high long-term success rate, but they are not guaranteed to succeed in every case. Understanding why implants can fail, and what factors increase risk, is relevant for patients in New York who are evaluating whether implants are appropriate for their situation.

## What Implant Failure Means

Implant failure refers broadly to the implant not becoming stable in the jaw or failing after an initial period of successful integration. There are two general categories:

**Early failure** occurs during the healing phase, before or shortly after osseointegration — the process by which bone grows onto and bonds with the implant surface. The implant does not stabilize in the bone.

**Late failure** occurs after the implant appeared to have integrated successfully. This can happen months or years after placement and is often associated with infection, bone loss around the implant, or excessive mechanical stress.

## Common Contributing Factors

Several factors are associated with a higher risk of implant complications:

- **Insufficient bone volume or density** at the implant site affects whether the implant can achieve initial stability and integrate properly. This is why bone assessment — including imaging — is part of the treatment planning process.
- **Infection** at or around the implant site can prevent integration or destabilize a previously integrated implant. Peri-implantitis is a condition involving inflammation and bone loss around the implant.
- **Systemic health conditions** such as uncontrolled diabetes, conditions that affect bone metabolism, or immune system factors can reduce healing capacity.
- **Smoking** is associated with reduced blood flow to tissue and bone, which affects healing.
- **Mechanical overload** — placing more force on an implant than the surrounding bone can support — can contribute to long-term failure.
- **Poor placement or prosthetic design** can create conditions that lead to bone loss or mechanical failure over time.

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

- What imaging and bone assessment will be performed before implant placement?
- How do you evaluate whether I am an appropriate candidate for implants given my health history?
- What is your protocol if an implant does not integrate?
- Is a bone graft or other augmentation needed at this site before placement?
- How should I monitor for signs of a problem after placement?
