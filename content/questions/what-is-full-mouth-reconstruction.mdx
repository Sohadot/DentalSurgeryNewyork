## Overview

Full mouth reconstruction is a treatment planning concept, not a single procedure. It refers to a coordinated treatment plan that addresses multiple missing, damaged, or failing teeth simultaneously — typically involving oral surgery, restorative dentistry, and sometimes periodontal treatment as part of a staged sequence.

The term is frequently encountered in patient research but can be difficult to interpret because it is used with significant variation by different providers and marketing contexts.

## What It Typically Involves

A full mouth reconstruction plan may include some combination of:

- tooth extractions (individual or multiple)
- bone grafting to restore jaw volume where needed
- dental implant placement (multiple implants or full-arch systems)
- the restorative component — crowns, bridges, or implant-supported prosthetics
- periodontal treatment if gum disease is present
- temporary restorations during treatment stages

The specific combination depends on how many teeth are involved, what bone support exists, what the patient's periodontal and systemic health situation is, and what the restoration goal is.

## How It Differs From a Single Implant

A single dental implant replaces one tooth in a straightforward sequence: implant placement, osseointegration, abutment, crown. Full mouth reconstruction is more complex because:

- multiple implants may need to be staged around the bone grafting sequence
- bone volume may need to be built in multiple areas before implant placement
- the provisional (temporary) restoration plan requires coordination
- multiple specialists may be involved — oral surgery for extractions and implant placement, periodontics for gum treatment, restorative dentistry for the final prosthetics

## Relationship to All-on-4 and Full-Arch Systems

Full mouth reconstruction is sometimes used interchangeably with full-arch implant systems like All-on-4, but they are not the same concept. All-on-4 is a specific protocol for restoring an entire arch on four strategically placed implants. Full mouth reconstruction is a broader category that may or may not use an All-on-4 approach depending on bone support and the number of teeth involved.

## Cost Complexity

Full mouth reconstruction is typically among the more complex cost estimates to evaluate because:

- multiple providers may be involved, each billing separately
- the treatment stages span many months
- bone grafting, which affects both cost and timeline, varies significantly by extent needed
- the restoration component — whether fixed bridge, implant-supported denture, or individual crowns — is a major cost driver

Written itemization and a staged treatment plan with associated costs at each stage are essential for understanding a full mouth reconstruction quote.

## Questions to Ask a Provider

- What specifically is included in this treatment plan?
- Who performs which components — surgery, periodontics, restoration?
- What is the sequencing and timeline?
- What happens if bone grafting reveals more or less bone loss than expected?
- What does the provisional restoration look like and what does it cost?
- Is the final restoration a fixed bridge, individual crowns, or implant-supported prosthetic — and why?
