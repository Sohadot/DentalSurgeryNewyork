## Introduction

Active gum disease affects dental implant candidacy. Understanding the relationship between periodontal health and implant success helps patients in New York know what needs to be addressed before implant treatment can proceed — and what questions to ask during consultation.

---

## Why Gum Disease Matters for Implants

Dental implants depend on healthy surrounding tissue for long-term stability. Active gum disease — particularly periodontitis, which involves infection of the bone and tissue structures supporting teeth — creates conditions that significantly increase implant failure risk.

Two primary mechanisms explain why:

**Bacterial environment** — Periodontal disease is associated with elevated levels of pathogenic bacteria in the oral environment. These same bacteria are implicated in peri-implantitis, the infection of tissue surrounding a dental implant. Placing an implant into an environment with unresolved periodontal infection substantially raises the risk of early failure.

**Bone loss at the site** — Periodontitis causes loss of the alveolar bone supporting teeth. If the tooth being replaced was lost due to periodontal disease, the surrounding bone may be insufficient for implant placement without prior bone grafting.

---

## Active Gum Disease vs. Treated Gum Disease

The distinction that matters most is whether gum disease is active or treated and controlled:

**Active periodontitis** — Ongoing infection with continuing bone loss. This must be resolved before implant placement. Most providers will not place implants in the presence of active periodontal disease.

**Treated and controlled periodontal disease** — Gum disease that has been treated and is now stable. Implant candidacy depends on the degree of bone loss, the health of remaining tissue, and ongoing maintenance compliance. Many patients with a history of periodontal disease do receive implants successfully.

**Mild gingivitis** — Inflammation of gum tissue without bone involvement. This is a lower bar to address before implant placement and does not by itself preclude candidacy.

---

## What Must Happen Before Implants Can Proceed

If gum disease is present, the typical sequence before implant placement includes:

1. Periodontal evaluation — probing depths, bone loss assessment via imaging
2. Active treatment — scaling and root planing, periodontal surgery if indicated
3. Re-evaluation — confirming disease has been arrested and tissue has stabilized
4. Bone volume assessment — CBCT imaging to determine if sufficient bone exists at the intended implant site
5. Bone grafting if needed — to restore volume lost to periodontal disease before implant placement

The timeline for this sequence can extend several months before implant placement is appropriate.

---

## Long-Term Maintenance After Implants

Patients with a history of periodontal disease face elevated risk of peri-implantitis — infection around the implant that can lead to bone loss and implant failure over time. More frequent maintenance appointments are commonly recommended for this patient group.

---

## Questions to Ask Your Provider

- Is my gum disease active, or has it been treated and is now controlled?
- What specific treatment is needed before implant placement can be considered, and what is the timeline?
- Has bone loss from periodontal disease affected the implant site — and will grafting be required?
- What maintenance schedule do you recommend post-implant given my periodontal history?
